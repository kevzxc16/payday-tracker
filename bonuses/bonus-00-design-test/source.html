Typography & structure

01 / 04
Section eyebrow — small caps

A section heading at 13 point

Body type is set at 11 point with a 1.5 leading. That's deliberate — anything smaller and printed PDFs become a strain to read on paper. Hierarchy is carried by weight and size, not color, so the document still works when somebody prints it in black and white on their office laser printer.

A subhead at 10.5 point

And another paragraph underneath. This is what bold text looks like, used sparingly for the words that actually need to stand out.

Muted text — a half-step quieter than body.

Numbers like $1,234.56 use tabular figures so columns of money line up vertically. Same for tracking numbers: 01 · 02 · 03.

Callouts
A standard callout

Used to mark a "stop and read this" zone. Warm paper background, navy left-rule. Don't use these for everything — they lose weight when overused.

A warning callout

Used when something deserves caution but isn't an emergency.

A danger callout

Reserved for "don't do this" content. Red-leaning, restrained.

Checklists & fill-ins

02 / 04

Standard checklist

  • Move savings before you spend The moment your paycheck lands, transfer the savings amount first. Out of sight, out of mind.
  • Pay priority bills Rent, utilities, insurance. The bills that hurt most if they go unpaid.
  • Set your discretionary amount What's left is what you can spend without panic. Write it somewhere visible.

Numbered steps

  1. Open the app. Check what's coming due before payday.
  2. Run the reset. Move money, mark bills, log what's left.
  3. Close the loop. Note one thing you learned from the last period.
